title: The Long Tables
part: The second, third and fourth days · 1/B Good Death, 7 April 2023

## The second day

The second day was still low-key. People kept arriving all day, more than a trickle and less than a
flood. There were no set meals, only food that never ran out, and clusters of people sitting down to
it around mealtimes. There were pickup games and gambling and gossip. Children ran underfoot. Cousins
who had not spoken in years caught up, and those who were looking flirted with those who were looking
back.

The woman Daatsu had recommended mushroom steaks to was a cook named Ninosh. She kept coming back
round, talking about her cooking and asking where his food came from. Each time he had something
different on the plate, and each time it was the same conversation from a new angle. She was also
plainly interested in him. He did not notice.

There is no alcohol at a gathering like this, but there are drinks, mostly brewed from herbs and
fungus, that most people take the way others take weak beer. They leave a glow at the edges of things
and loosen the tongue. Migatuka was slightly buzzed on medicine and one of them. She overheard enough,
and finally called it out loud: Ninosh wanted to sleep with him.

Ninosh turned to her and laughed. "Was I that obvious?"

"Oh, honey," Migatuka said.

Ninosh blushed and turned back to Daatsu. "Seek me out if you're so inclined," she said, and made her
exit.

A little later Migatuka ran into Kaptaanzi, a colleague she had never got on with. Kaptaanzi was still
working. Migatuka was not.

"Hey, bitch," Migatuka said. "How's medical?"

"Better, recently."

"I'm glad I'm not there."

"I can see why," Kaptaanzi said. "I guess we shouldn't be expecting you back anytime soon. Don't worry
about it, we're good." Migatuka ordered another. Daatsu went and found her a plate of ordinary
mushrooms, the kind that are only food.

Wazawi was glad to have finished her adulthood ritual, but she felt out of place with both the
children and the adults. The chaos of the day, with nothing to hold on to, was stressful. She went
out into the woods of the big park in the middle of the city, where you can lose sight of the buildings
even though a highway is a mile and a half away, and used her new privileges to camp out under the
stars. She did not ask anyone, and she did not tell anyone.

Syn had found, after meeting Grandmother, that they felt safe. They were where they were supposed to
be, so they could stop hunting and let themselves enjoy the place. They talked to everyone, loudly and
awkwardly. They kept coming back to Daatsu's table for more ("You got the real good snacks, you know
what I'm talking about"), and Daatsu greeted them each time with *oh, you again.* The uncles,
Tisimiga, Ninosanipan and Hokawichakun, had had their game of naasi going for a day and a half, and
they pulled Syn in to blow on the dice for luck. Syn never took a seat at the table, but threw for
whichever of them asked. Once they threw two twelves.

For Soova the second day was the last day to get ready; the next few would not allow it. She was
irritated that Wazawi had disappeared when there was family work to be done. The medicines and
preparations had been made ahead of time. What was left was to set everything out, to ready and
cleanse the room where Grandmother would sit, and to meditate and pray herself into the right frame of
mind. Kooyoopi, who would attend Grandmother with her, had already been through and laid their own
materials out with care. Soova worked through hers slowly, knowing it would all be disturbed in a few
days, and found it quieted her. Then she went to bed.

## The third day

Wazawi was back, trying to help and very interested in everything.

The third day started late. By late morning the tables had been pushed together into a rough U, with
Grandmother at the centre and her immediate family on either side. It was loud, and every so often
someone stood and it quieted. A person would repeat a piece of wisdom Grandmother had given them at a
turning point in their life, or tell a story about trouble they got into as a child and how she called
them on it. Tawihichik, of the Council of Twelve, came for a few hours and spoke about Grandmother's
good influence on the community.

Soova stood up. She said how meaningful it had been to learn from Grandmother: how to raise children,
and how to honour your feelings without letting them dictate what you do.

There was a break for a rest in the middle of the afternoon. In the evening there would be a feast,
and Daatsu was pulled in to help with the preparations.

Before the feast came the gift-giving. Syn and Daatsu, and others from the wider circle, were given
small handmade trinkets.

Migatuka was given a folded blanket. She unfolded it and burst into tears, and held it close. It had
been Zibizin's. Zibizin's falcon was still there, plain to see, but it had been reworked to look like
a fox. Syn saw, and came over to comfort her. When she could, Migatuka put the blanket away in the
cubby where her things were. The evening had lost its joy for her.

Soova was given a wooden case. Inside was a beautiful set of jewellery, an heirloom, the kind that
would have gone to Grandmother's daughter if she had ever had one. Soova would never have presumed. It
settled something for her: she was accepted, a part of this family.

"Would it be okay if I wore these during your ceremonies?" she asked.

"I would be honoured if you would," Grandmother said.

"I can't tell you how much this means to me."

Then they ate. There were smoked eggs. Nothing would start until the next afternoon, so that everyone
could sleep late.

## The fourth day

Once things got going on the fourth day, the family told Grandmother's life. Three or four
storytellers passed it between them. Her childhood came out sparse and vague, a little cryptic. In her
adolescence she arrived in Cahokia with the man who became her husband. He had died about fifteen
years ago, and his death had been a quieter affair.

It was obvious to Syn that something had been left out. They came away with a strong feeling that it
was something too unsafe even to mention: something in Grandmother's relationship to the Black, to the
void, that she was trying to escape, or to hide.

Then came the speeches. Soova spoke about how Grandmother's lost child had helped her with the loss of
her own, and how not to get lost in grief.

It was the first Seeing at which Wazawi had been present for this part, and she listened intently. The
early parts of the story felt strange to her, familiar and hard to picture at once; they had happened
nearly a hundred years ago.

They went out to watch the sun set, and then Grandmother withdrew. The evening ended in quieter
conversations, as small groups peeled off and built fires. Wazawi asked Soova whether she could take
part in the next few days, or watch. Soova chided her: she could help afterwards, and only afterwards.

## Grandmother's request

At some point in the evening each of them was sought out and told that Grandmother wished to speak
with them. They gathered, a little confused. Fur curtains parted, and Grandmother came in.

"Thank you," she said. "I have a request I ask of you. It is not something that can be done alone,
which is why I ask this of all of you."

She took Syn's hands. "We have only just met, but you have the eyes that can see what needs to be
seen. But the understanding will come from others. I trust your sight."

She moved to Soova and clasped her hands. "Like you, I do not come from Cahokia originally. Few know
that, during the All Tribes War, my husband found me on his way back. I have not been to Diné since I
was a child, but I have something that I need returned to my ancestral lands." She turned and handed
Soova a sealed box. "I ask that after I am gone you would bring this back to its home."

She turned to Daatsu. "Your family speaks highly of you, and have consented to loaning you to me. Your
skills are highly praised. I have a song for you." She sang it: an old song-map, the kind once used to
cross great stretches of plain. When it came round to the refrain and began to repeat, Daatsu sang
with her. When they finished she said, "Thank you for guiding my daughter back home."

Then she came to Migatuka. "Soova will need a healer. She can heal others, but she must not neglect
herself. You are to make sure she is able to return in safety and health. Would you do that for me?"
Migatuka said she would.

Grandmother dismissed them all but Soova.

Syn wanted nothing to do with the box. It felt dangerous to them, or sacred. To Daatsu it was cargo;
the song was the complication. It went further west than he had ever been, deep into the Diné
Republic. Song-maps had not been used in a generation or two, and it was unusual that he knew them at
all.

When they were alone, Grandmother told Soova, "What I told Migatuka is only half true. She is
struggling, and needs a healer of the soul. Time to recuperate, and space away from temptations, may be
of help to her. I hope you two can be of aid to one another."

That night Wazawi crept in, very determined, and hid in a closet, where she slept standing up so that
she would be there in the morning. No one saw her.
