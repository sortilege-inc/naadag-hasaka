## Gambling with Naasi

One of my adversary NPC's has the Addiction (gambling) burden, and is going to secretly attempt to wager the evidence against the party that they are being hunted for against one of the PC's surrendering. As such, I made rules for using Naasii as a gambling game in Coyote and Crow proper:
Naasii is played between 2-4 players.
- All players agree on each player’s stake. Normally, this is an even nizi stake, but items and favors can also be wagered. All games of Naasii are winner-takes-all
- The game takes place over 2-5 rounds. Winner is the first person who wins two rounds.
- First roller is determined by a single D12 roll, the highest number goes first, play proceeds clockwise until all players have scored or busted that round. In the event of a tie on first player roll, players keep rolling until one rolls a higher number
- On their turn, the roller rolls 3 white dice. They are attempting to make runs (numbers in ascending order) or sets (instances of the same number). After rolling their dice, they have the option of scoring their dice or rolling again.
a. If they roll again, they lock at least one of their dice, then pick up their unlocked dice and reroll them. To that roll, they add 2 white dice and 1 black die. They then have the option of scoring or rolling a 3rd time. They can roll again after that a 4th and final time. Each time they roll, the reroll the unlocked dice and add 2 white and 1 black die. Any white die showing a 12 is wild
i. Characters may use Legendary Ranks and Mind to increase or decrease the value of any white die in any roll after black dice are resolved. White dice cannot be increased to a 12, and black dice cannot be affected
b. In rolls 2-4, before scoring or rolling again, each player must resolve their black dice. If any black die shows a 12, the player BUSTS and their turn ends with no points scored unless they also show a 12 among their unlocked dice. Otherwise, the player may not score one die matching their black die (but may score all but one die if multiple white dice show that number). A Bust may be avoided if the player has also rolled a white 12 to counter it.
i. An unscrupulous player may also attempt to cheat their way out of a Bust using a Skulduggery check. The SN is the highest Mental Defense of their opponents with the following modifiers:
1. +1 SN for each opponent beyond the first
2. +1 SN for every 3 neutral or opposed onlookers
a. Each +1 can be counteracted with a success from a simultaneous Charm or Deception as a distraction by an allied onlooker. A critical success may be used to remove a +1 from an opponent beyond the first, or give -1 SN if there are only 2 players
3. +1 SN for every other Bust a player has averted this game using this method
ii. On a success, the player may change their black die showing a 12 to match one of their unlocked white dice. On a critical success, they may change it to any number. On a failure, they still Bust. On a critical failure, the players attempt to cheat is discovered by their opponents.
- The winner of the round is the player who has the most dice scored as part of a set or run. In the case of a tie, each player rolls their unscored white dice again simultaneously and scores them to their run or set. Continue until one player has more scored dice than another.
a. If two or more players score a set or run of 9 dice, the round restarts with only those players participating
b. If all players before the final player in a round have busted, the last player may score a run or set of 1 on their first roll to win the round.
- In the situation that all players have won 1 round, the last round occurs simultaneously. All players roll at the same time, resolve any black dice, then secretly select one of their white or black dice that has yet to be rolled. They reveal the selected dice simultaneously. A white die designates that the player will score their dice, a black die designates that the player will roll again
a. In the case that all remaining players bust on the same roll during a simultaneous round, all dice in that roll are re-rolled.
- Sidebets are popular in games of Naasii as well. These are played in a pool system, and usually overseen by the house in gambling dens. Onlookers pay into a pool, usually a 10, 50, or 100 nizi buy-in, and are assigned a number. A tracker notes all numbers rolled every roll (if it matters, this occurs in the step after Legendary Ranks and Mind are assigned but before dice are scored/locked) and keeps a running tally. Numbers are divided as evenly as possible, and the pool is split evenly with the winners (or co-winners if two or more numbers are at the top). In professional gambling dens, it is customary for the house to always take 12 and no payout be awarded to patrons should 12 be the most common roll.
